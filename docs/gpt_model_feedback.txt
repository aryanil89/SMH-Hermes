# 8. Main user workflows to build

## Workflow 1: “Should I care?”

This is the most important one.

### Input

* Temperature slightly rising
* Storage latency slightly rising
* No major alert yet

### Hermes output

```text
Current risk: Medium

Why it matters:
Rack B1 temperature is rising and storage latency started increasing within the same time window.

Current confidence:
62%

Recommended action:
Continue monitoring for 5 minutes before escalating unless leak or power signal appears.
```

### Why this is strong

* Monitoring tools show metrics.
* Hermes answers whether the engineer should care.

***

## Workflow 2: Root cause analysis

### User asks

```text
Why are backup jobs taking longer?
```

### Hermes checks

* Storage telemetry
* Network telemetry
* Compute telemetry
* Environmental sensor state
* Recent incident timeline

### Hermes answers

```text
Likely root cause:
Cooling degradation affecting Rack B1 storage nodes.

Evidence:
- Temperature increased from 24°C to 34°C.
- Storage latency increased from 12 ms to 68 ms.
- Backup throughput dropped by 18%.
- Network packet loss remains normal.

Confidence:
81%

Recommended action:
Inspect cooling or airflow around Rack B1 before restarting backup jobs.
```

### Why this is strong

* It connects physical and digital symptoms.
* It explains reasoning.
* It is realistic for infrastructure operations.

***

## Workflow 3: Cost of ignoring

### User asks

```text
What happens if we ignore this for 30 minutes?
```

### Hermes answers

```text
If ignored for 30 minutes:

Expected impact:
- Storage latency may continue increasing.
- Backup jobs may miss the SLA window.
- Host thermal throttling risk may increase.
- Incident severity may move from Medium to High.

Recommended action:
Investigate cooling or obstruction now.
```

### Why this is innovative

* Most dashboards say what happened.
* Hermes predicts operational consequence.

***

## Workflow 4: Explain your reasoning

### User asks

```text
Why do you think cooling is the issue?
```

### Hermes answers

```text
Reasoning:

1. Temperature increased first.
2. Storage latency increased shortly after.
3. Network metrics stayed normal.
4. Compute load did not spike.
5. Similar pattern usually indicates environmental impact rather than network congestion.

Conclusion:
Cooling degradation is the most likely cause.
```

### Why this matters

* Builds trust.
* Judges will like explainability.
* Makes it feel like a senior engineer, not a black-box chatbot.
